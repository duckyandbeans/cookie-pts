I. Tenses in Context

Read the dialogue to answer questions 1-4:

Nina: Are you excited for the school trip to Japan?
Leo: Definitely! 1. I __________ my passport renewal form last Tuesday.
Nina: Good. 2. __________ your flight tickets yet? The prices are going up.
Leo: Yes, my dad handled it. He 3. __________ to Tokyo several times before we were even born, so he knows the best airlines.
Nina: Awesome. By this time next month, we 4. __________ sushi in Shibuya!
1. Choose the correct verb for blank (1):
  • A. have submitted
  • B. submitted
  • C. am submitting
  • D. had submitted
  • E. submit
Answer: B. submitted (Past Simple - indicates a completed action with a specific past time "last Tuesday").
2. Choose the correct verb for blank (2):
  • A. Do you book
  • B. Did you book
  • C. Have you booked
  • D. Are you booking
  • E. Will you book
Answer: C. Have you booked (Present Perfect - asking about a recently completed action relevant to the present "yet").
3. Choose the correct verb for blank (3):
  • A. has traveled
  • B. travels
  • C. traveled
  • D. had traveled
  • E. was traveling
Answer: D. had traveled (Past Perfect - an action that happened before another point in the past "before we were born").
4. Choose the correct verb for blank (4):
  • A. will have eaten
  • B. are eating
  • C. eat
  • D. will be eating
  • E. have eaten
Answer: D. will be eating (Future Continuous - an ongoing action at a specific time in the future).

III. Modal Verbs

7. To ensure everyone's safety in the chemistry lab, students ___ wear protective goggles at all times.
  • A. might
  • B. should
  • C. shouldn't
  • D. mustn't
  • E. can
Answer: B. should (Expresses strong advice or a rule. *Must* is also acceptable if present, but *should* is the best fit here).
8. Passengers ___ unfasten their seatbelts while the airplane is experiencing turbulence.
  • A. don't have to
  • B. mustn't
  • C. should
  • D. might not
  • E. ought to
Answer: B. mustn't (Expresses a strict prohibition).

VII. Reading Comprehension (Historical Recount)

The Industrial Revolution, beginning in the late 18th century, marked a massive transition from agrarian, handcraft economies to industry and machine manufacturing. As large factories sprang up across Britain, factory owners sought cheap labor to maximize their profits. Consequently, thousands of children were recruited from orphanages and poor families. These children were highly valued because their small hands could easily reach into tight spaces between operating machines to fix broken threads or clear jams. However, this came at a severe cost. Children worked gruelling 12 to 14-hour shifts in dangerous, unventilated buildings. It wasn't until the Factory Acts of the 1830s that the government finally intervened, setting age limits and reducing work hours, which eventually paved the way for modern labor laws.
18. What is the main idea of the text?
  • A. The Industrial Revolution completely destroyed Britain's economy.
  • B. Children were the primary inventors of factory machines.
  • C. The Industrial Revolution caused a harsh shift in labor, notably exploiting children before laws intervened.
  • D. The Factory Acts were created to increase the working hours of adults.
  • E. Farming remained the most important industry throughout the 18th century.
Answer: C (Captures the core theme: industrial shift, child labor exploitation, and eventual legal reform).
19. According to the text, why were children specifically valued by factory owners?
  • A. They had better engineering degrees than adults.
  • B. Their small hands allowed them to fix machines in tight spaces.
  • C. They were stronger and could lift heavier materials.
  • D. They demanded higher wages which improved the economy.
  • E. They brought their own tools to the factories.
Answer: B (Directly stated in the text regarding their physical size).
20. What action did the government take in the 1830s?
  • A. They built more orphanages for the workers.
  • B. They banned all machines in British factories.
  • C. They set age limits and reduced working hours for children.
  • D. They forced children to work 14-hour shifts.
  • E. They replaced child workers with advanced robots.
Answer: C (Through the Factory Acts, they intervened to protect children).

X. Reading Comprehension (Descriptive/Recount)

Scuba diving is vastly different from snorkeling. While snorkeling keeps you on the surface of the water, scuba diving allows you to explore the depths of the ocean using a heavy tank of compressed air strapped to your back. Last summer, I took my first certification course in Bali. Before jumping in, the instructor made sure we understood how to use the regulator, which is the mouthpiece that delivers air. We also had to wear a tight neoprene wetsuit. Even in tropical waters, your body loses heat quickly, and the wetsuit acts as a crucial layer of insulation. As I descended into the coral reef, I felt a wave of anxiety, but it was quickly replaced by awe. Gliding alongside sea turtles and colorful schools of fish in complete silence was the most magical experience of my life. I am already saving up for my next dive trip.
25. What distinguishes scuba diving from snorkeling according to the text?
  • A. Scuba diving requires you to hold your breath.
  • B. Scuba diving uses a tank of compressed air to go deep underwater.
  • C. Snorkeling is only done in cold water.
  • D. Scuba diving doesn't require any gear.
  • E. Snorkeling uses a regulator mouthpiece.
Answer: B (Uses compressed air to explore depths).
26. Why did the writer have to wear a neoprene wetsuit?
  • A. To protect against shark attacks.
  • B. Because it makes the diver swim much faster.
  • C. To provide insulation and prevent the body from losing heat quickly.
  • D. To help the heavy air tank float.
  • E. Because it is required by Bali law.
Answer: C (Acts as a crucial layer of insulation).
27. How did the writer's emotions change during the dive?
  • A. From excitement to extreme boredom.
  • B. From anxiety to awe and amazement.
  • C. From anger to sadness.
  • D. They remained completely calm the entire time.
  • E. From happiness to terror.
Answer: B ("felt a wave of anxiety, but it was quickly replaced by awe").
