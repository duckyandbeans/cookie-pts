1. Neutral & Factual Verbs

Used to present information simply, without strong feelings or special bias.

State
Menyatakan
To write or say something clearly and directly.
Describe
Mendeskripsikan
To give details about what something looks like or how it happened.
Observe
Mengamati
To look at something carefully and note down what you see.
Examine
Memeriksa / Meneliti
To study something closely to look for errors or hidden information.
Illustrate
Mengilustrasikan / Memberi contoh
To make an idea easy to understand by sharing an example or a picture.

Checkpoint Quiz 1: Neutral Verbs

Question 1.1: The author uses a simple chart to show how a school grew over 10 years. The author wants to make the numbers easy. The author is using the chart to ________ the growth.
Question 1.2: A scientist uses a microscope for two weeks to study tiny cells and find out why they look different. The scientist is trying to ________ the cells.
Question 1.3: A witness tells the police exactly what happened step-by-step during a car crash, explaining the colors and positions of the cars. The witness is ________ the accident.
Question 1.4: An astronomer looks at the night sky, notices a quick flash of light on his screen, and immediately writes down the time. The astronomer ________ the light.
Question 1.5: Rule 1 in the classroom guide says: "All students must raise their hands to speak." The guide clearly ________ the rule.
Question 1.6: To help readers understand how bad the weather was, a reporter tells the true story of a farmer who lost all his crops. The reporter uses this story to ________ the power of the storm.

2. Argumentative & Assertive Verbs

Used when the author wants to express a strong opinion or defend an idea.

Argue
Berargumen
To share reasons or facts to prove that your idea is correct.
Assert
Menegaskan
To state an opinion confidently and strongly, even without proof yet.
Emphasize
Menekankan
To make an important word or idea stand out so readers notice it.
Advocate
Menganjurkan / Mendukung
To publicly recommend or support a particular cause or policy action.
Contend
Mempertahankan pendapat
To hold onto your opinion firmly during an active debate or argument.

Checkpoint Quiz 2: Argumentative Verbs

Question 2.1: A green activist writes a letter to the city government, strongly asking them to ban plastic bags in supermarkets immediately. The activist is trying to ________ a new rule.
Question 2.2: A politician stands up and says, "Our town is the safest town in the country!" He does not show any police data or numbers. He just says it very loudly and confidently. The politician is ________ his belief.
Question 2.3: In a long paper, a student uses graphs, historical data, and interviews to prove that extra homework improves test scores. The student ________ that homework is helpful.
Question 2.4: During the safety briefing, the teacher speaks very loudly, uses bold red letters on the slide, and says "Warning!" three times. The teacher is trying to ________ the safety rule.
Question 2.5: Even though the other debate team presents strong points against him, the speaker refuses to change his mind. He stands firm and says his plan is still the best. The speaker ________ that he is right.
Question 2.6: A group of parents starts a petition and goes to city hall to ask for a new library down the street. The parents are ________ for a new library.

3. Tentative & Speculative Verbs

Used when the author is guessing, sharing a hypothesis, or is not 100% sure yet.

Suggest
Mengisyaratkan / Menyarankan
To share an idea gently, or hint at an answer without direct proof.
Hypothesize
Menghipotesiskan
To create a testable, scientific guess before starting an experiment.
Speculate
Berspekulasi / Menduga
To guess blindly based on rumors, feelings, or very little information.
Imply
Menyiratkan
To hint at a message between the lines without saying it out loud.
Postulate
Mendalilkan
To set a foundational rule or assume something is true so you can begin your logic.

Practice: Tentative Verbs

Question 3.1: A police officer sees a tiny scratch on a door. He does not have full proof yet, but he writes: "It is possible that a small tool was used here." The officer is ________.
Question 3.2: Before mixing the liquids, the chemistry team writes a guess: "Adding heat will make this bubble." Now, they start 5 tests to see if their guess is right. The team is ________.
Question 3.3: A critic writes: "The manager bought a luxury yacht this week, while his workers didn't receive their pay on time." The critic never calls the manager bad, but the critic is ________ that the manager is selfish.
Question 3.4: In a quiet voice, a student says: "The sky is very dark; perhaps it will rain during our lunch break." The student is ________ that they change their plans.
Question 3.5: To build a new math theory, scientists assume that a perfect circle has no rough edges. They do not test this every day; they just accept it as a basic rule so they can start their math. The scientists ________ this basic rule.
Question 3.6: Without checking any official facts, a podcaster guesses that a new phone will explode based on an unverified internet rumor. The podcaster is ________.

4. Opposing & Critical Verbs

Used when the author wants to disagree, prove an idea wrong, or show a conflict.

Refute
Membantah / Mematahkan
To completely prove that an idea is false by using clear, solid facts or evidence.
Dispute
Memperdebatkan / Menyanggah
To question if a fact or a source is true, honest, or correct.
Concede
Mengakui kebenaran / Mengalah
To agree that your opponent has a good or correct point, usually before you continue your own argument.
Dismiss
Mengabaikan / Menepis
To refuse to listen to or consider an idea because you think it is completely silly or lacks value.
Reject
Menolak
To say an absolute "no" to a contract, offer, or new idea.

Paragraph 1:
Hari ini, migrasi internasional adalah topik utama dalam geopolitik kontemporer. Gerakan lintas batas yang masif ini sekarang sangat meluas karena terjadi di hampir setiap negara. Ini menjadi tema dominan dalam wacana global saat ini, dan mengubah populasi lokal di semua negara penerima. Di masa lalu, premis dasar utama dari hukum global sangatlah sederhana. Pemerintah percaya bahwa tembok perbatasan akan berhasil mengatur dan mengendalikan perubahan struktural yang tiba-tiba ini. Namun, bahkan dengan kerangka kerja kebijakan yang ketat ini, imigrasi global terus tumbuh. Jumlah total migran telah meningkat ke tingkat yang belum pernah terjadi sebelumnya, melewati ambang batas sejarah yang baru. Perpindahan penduduk yang besar ini memaksa negara-negara bangsa modern untuk mengelola lingkungan yang sangat beragam. Setiap tahun, seorang migran menghadapi banyak hambatan hukum, aturan yang sulit, dan pos pemeriksaan birokrasi yang lambat hanya untuk membangun kehidupan yang layak dan dapat bertahan hidup.

Paragraph 2:
Respon politik domestik terhadap migrasi jarang bersatu karena warga negara memiliki pendapat yang sangat berbeda. Sebaliknya, sementara beberapa kelompok fokus sepenuhnya pada bantuan kemanusiaan, kelompok lain khawatir tentang beban infrastruktur yang parah. Mereka percaya bahwa pertumbuhan populasi yang cepat menempatkan beban yang mendalam pada aparatus kesehatan lokal dan memenuhi semua program perumahan kota yang tersedia. Dalam perdebatan ini, para pemimpin lokal mencoba melindungi setiap sumber daya komunitas dan secara akurat menilai pasar tenaga kerja. Namun, banyak ekonom berpendapat bahwa ketakutan kehilangan pekerjaan sebenarnya kekurangan bukti nyata. Ketika integrasi direncanakan dengan baik, pekerja baru membantu ekonomi lokal tumbuh, sehingga kekhawatiran tentang penggantian posisi ekonomi pekerja asli menjadi sepenuhnya berlebihan.

Paragraph 3:
Anehnya (secara paradoks), rezim perbatasan yang terlalu ketat secara tidak sengaja dapat membuat keluarga yang melarikan diri menggunakan jaringan penyelundupan manusia yang berbahaya untuk menyeberangi perbatasan. Jika jalur pemrosesan hukum bergerak terlalu lambat, kantor perbatasan mengalami tumpukan antrean yang tidak normal. Keterlambatan sistem ini sepenuhnya menghentikan kemajuan manusia dan menjebak orang-orang yang rentan dalam ketidakpastian hukum yang menyakitkan selama berbulan-bulan. Memperbaiki masalah hak asasi manusia yang besar ini akan memerlukan perombakan total secara struktural untuk menciptakan dampak yang mendalam dan positif pada hukum migrasi global. Mengubah hukum ini tetap menjadi tugas yang penting, meskipun sangat sensitif, bagi pemerintah demokratis modern.

3. Classroom Speaking Game: The Drama Circle

2 ex best friends are now in a messy breakup. They are gossiping about each other to their mutual friend (you!). Read their lines out loud, report them to your partner, and then check the box when you are done. You can also click the hint button to see academic reporting verb options that fit each line!

Student A: Cassandra's Task

Read your lines line-by-line out loud to your partner. Convert them to reported speech together, check the box, and review your reporting verb options!

Line 1: "OMG girl, I am telling you Leonardo is definitely cheating on you with Miranda."
|
Academic Options: asserts, claims, alleges, contends
Example: Cassandra claims that Leonardo is cheating on Lisa.
Line 2: "I saw him walk out of the movie theater with her yesterday."
|
Academic Options: states, notes, observes, reports
Example: She states that she witnessed him walking out of the theater.
Line 3: "This is so crazy because you guys are going to get married soon!"
|
Academic Options: exclaims, remarks, comments, points out
Example: Cassandra exclaims that the situation is wild given their wedding plan.
Line 4: "I actually scream at the both of them once I was close enough to them."
|
Academic Options: admits, describes, recounts, states
Example: She recounts shouting at both individuals in public.
Line 5: "I told them you guys are so shameless and that Leonardo doesn't deserve you."
|
Academic Options: criticizes, insults, slams, dismisses
Example: She openly criticizes Leonardo's actions and character.

Student B: Lisa's Task

Read your lines line-by-line out loud to your partner. Convert them to reported speech together, check the box, and review your reporting verb options!

Line 1: "Shut up, Cassandra stop lying. Leonardo was literally by my side yesterday."
|
Academic Options: refutes, disputes, rejects, denies
Example: Lisa immediately refutes Cassandra's tracking claims.
Line 2: "Anastasia has told me all about you and that you're trying to break us up just to have him."
|
Academic Options: reveals, exposes, counters, discloses
Example: Lisa reveals that Anastasia uncovered Cassandra's secret intentions.
Line 3: "I thought you were my best friend and I thought that I could trust you, but you're just a two face backstabbing degenerate."
|
Academic Options: condemns, attacks, brands, labels
Example: She completely condemns Cassandra as a dishonest peer.
Line 4: "I literally took a photo with Leonardo yesterday so you need to stop lying."
|
Academic Options: demonstrates, illustrates, proves, corroborates
Example: Lisa points to a photograph to corroborate her partner's presence.
Line 5: "You need to find a therapist. You're so awful."
|
Academic Options: suggests, urges, recommends, advises
Example: Lisa sharply suggests that Cassandra seek counseling.
