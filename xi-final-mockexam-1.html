Part 4: Reading Comprehension & Writing

Directions: Read the text about "A New Home" and answer questions 21 - 31.
A New Home: Global Demographics

Paragraph 1: Today, moving to a new country is a common part of global demography. This cross-border movement is everywhere because people migrate for many different reasons. Historically, governments believed they could regulate these structural shifts simply by using strict borders. However, even with strong rules, global immigration keeps growing. The number of migrants has reached an unprecedented level. People leave their homes because of poverty, war, or geographic displacement. Every year, an asylum seeker navigates difficult laws and slow bureaucratic checkpoints to find safety.

Paragraph 2: The political reaction to these demographic changes is rarely unified. Conversely, some people welcome migrants, while others worry about infrastructure strain. They fear that a fast-growing population places a pervasive burden on local hospitals and municipal housing. However, many economists say these fears lack empirical support. When integration works well, foreign workers help the local economy grow. Therefore, worries about native workers losing their jobs are often superfluous.

Paragraph 3: Paradoxically, strict border rules can make the journey more dangerous for a fleeing family. When legal processing channels are slow, border offices suffer an anomalous backlog. This delay stalls human lives and traps vulnerable people in a legal limbo for months. To fix these human rights issues, governments must make profound overhauls to global migration policies. Changing these laws is a difficult, albeit very important, task for modern democracies.
